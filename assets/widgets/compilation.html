For the compiler a program is a point: its largest interaction J, set by the closest pair of atoms, and its largest amplitude Ωmax. The device accepts the rectangle J ≤ 1 (closest pair at rmin or further) and Ωmax ≤ 0.2268 (amplitude at the device maximum 2π × 2 MHz or below). Multiplying every energy by α and dividing time by α leaves the physics unchanged, so the compiler has exactly one free number: it moves the point along its line through the origin. Larger α means a shorter sequence, so it is worth pushing. The default profile keeps α = 1 and rejects any point outside the rectangle. The maximum-energy profile raises α until the point hits the boundary: drive-limited if the amplitude reaches the device maximum first, interaction-limited if the closest pair reaches rmin first. Drag the point, or move α yourself, to see which limit bites, and read off how long the sequence of T time units becomes. The detuning scales with α as well, and the device caps |δ| at 2π × 20 MHz: the compiler does not lower α for it, it rejects the program, so a large |δ| in the program can make the maximum-energy profile fail.


The device sets the energy unit Jmax = C6/(ħ rmin6) = 55.4 rad/µs, as in the earlier widgets, so one time unit is 18.05 ns.
