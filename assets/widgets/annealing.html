Knowing the ground state is one thing, reaching it is another. The register starts as the seven-atom one of the hands-on, driven at Ω = 0.15 so that its blockade graph has the unique maximum independent set {2, 3, 6, 7}. The drive ramps the amplitude up during the first fifth of T, holds it, and ramps it down during the last fifth; meanwhile the detuning starts at δ0 = −0.5, where every atom prefers g, and is swept to δf = +0.5, where the maximum independent set is the ground state. The only free number is T, which sets the velocity of the sweep. Too fast and the register never leaves |g⋯g⟩, as a single atom swept through resonance too quickly stays in g. A bit slower and excitations appear, scattered, some of them on neighbouring atoms: the interaction has not had the time to enforce the blockade. Slow enough and every likely outcome is an independent set, with the maximum one taking a share that keeps growing with T. Drag the atoms to change the graph, and the maximum independent set with it; Reset puts the register back.

H̃ = Σi<j J̃ij ni nj + Ω̃(t)/2 Σi Xi − δ̃(t) Σi ni with J̃ij = r̃ij−6 for every pair, tails included, integrated numerically, fixed-step fourth-order Runge–Kutta on the 27 amplitudes from |g⋯g⟩ with no approximation to the model. The detuning is swept at v = (δf − δ0)/(0.6 T). The probabilities are those of the final state, not shot frequencies. Starting coordinates from Stop 4 of chapter 2: neighbours at distance 1, every other pair at 1.72 or more, Rb = 0.15−1/6 = 1.37. A pair closer than r = 1 is drawn red, as the device would reject it; atoms stop at r = 0.6. The maximum independent sets are found by brute force; when there are several, P(MIS) sums over them and no atom is outlined. The empty set is an independent set, so at small T the independent-set curve is mostly |g⋯g⟩: the grey curve shows that share. The curve against T is recomputed when an atom is released.
