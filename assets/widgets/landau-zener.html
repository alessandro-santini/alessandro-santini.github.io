Two atoms at distance R are driven by the same amplitude Ω while the detuning is swept linearly from −δf to +δf in a time T, at the rate v = 2δf/T. Without the drive the levels |gg⟩, |ψ+⟩ and |rr⟩ cross at δ = 0 and at δ = C6/(ħR6), the blockade shift. The drive couples |gg⟩ to |ψ+⟩ and |ψ+⟩ to |rr⟩, and each crossing becomes an avoided crossing with gap √2 Ω. A slow sweep follows the ground state through the gaps; a fast one jumps across them with the Landau–Zener probability, which depends only on v/Ω². The rr level is named when it enters the plot; the dashed lines carry the colours of gg, ψ+ and rr. The dots on the energy levels show where the state is: each is as opaque as the population of its level, so a jump shows up as weight left on the upper branch after the gap. Where the pair ends up is set by the blockade shift: if δf < C6/(ħR6) the sweep stops before the second crossing and the ground state is the Bell state |ψ+⟩, while if δf > C6/(ħR6) the pair crosses the second gap too and both atoms end up excited. Drag the atoms apart, or move the R slider, to bring the second crossing into the window, and shorten T to see the jumps appear. The dashed line in the population plot is 1 − Pjump, the Landau–Zener prediction for the population that follows the ground state through one crossing.

In the symmetric subspace H/ħ has the matrix [[0, Ω/√2, 0], [Ω/√2, −δ, Ω/√2], [0, Ω/√2, −2δ + C6/(ħR6)]] in the basis {|gg⟩, |ψ+⟩, |rr⟩}, with C6/ħ = 865 723 rad µs−1 µm6; the antisymmetric state is never coupled. δ(t) = −δf + v t with v = 2δf/T. The two protocol panels show the drive: the amplitude is raised and lowered smoothly over the first and last stretch of detuning, 3Ω or 3√v from the ends whichever is wider, so that the drive is flat across the crossing region as the Landau–Zener formula assumes; with a drive switched on abruptly at a finite detuning, or a window so narrow that the two ramps meet before the drive is flat, the populations deviate from it; the detuning is a straight ramp. Pjump = exp(−π Ω²/|v|) for the two-level crossing of gap √2 Ω. When δf > C6/(ħR6) the pair sees two crossings, and following both gives P(rr) ≈ (1 − Pjump)². When C6/(ħR6) is comparable to Ω the two crossings overlap and the formula no longer applies. The disks in the sketch have radius Rb/2 with Rb = (C6/ħΩ)1/6: they overlap when the pair is blockaded, C6/(ħR6) > Ω. The dimensionless view measures lengths in units of rmin = 5 µm and energies in units of Jmax = C6/(ħ rmin6) = 55.4 rad/µs, as QoolQit does, so that the blockade shift is r−6.
