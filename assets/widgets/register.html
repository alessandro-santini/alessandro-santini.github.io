Each trap captures an atom independently with probability p, so a 6×6 target fills by chance alone with probability p36. Rearrangement moves atoms from the reservoir, the loaded traps outside the dashed square, into the empty target sites, one move at a time; each move can lose the atom, so cycles repeat until the target is complete. The default loss per move, 15 %, is about ten times the real figure, exaggerated so that losses show up within a few cycles; and the moves fly in straight lines through occupied traps, where a real tweezer follows row and column paths that avoid them.
