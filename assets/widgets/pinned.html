The global drive reaches every atom alike: a π-pulse at Ω = 0.1 takes both of these atoms from g to r. The detuning map modulator adds a per-atom term, a shared waveform Δ ≤ 0 scaled by a weight εi ∈ [0, 1] for each atom. Atom 1 carries the weight here, atom 2 none. Atom 1 therefore sees the detuning εΔ while the pulse is resonant for atom 2, and its Rabi oscillation becomes the detuned one of chapter 1: faster, and never reaching r. The further εΔ sits from resonance compared with Ω, the less the atom moves; at |εΔ| = 4 Ω it is excited a few times in a hundred, which is what "pinned" means. The DMM only ever lowers a detuning, so to favour one atom one pins the others.

Each atom follows the generalised Rabi formula Pr(t) = Ω²/(Ω² + δi²) sin²(√(Ω² + δi²) t/2) with δi = εiΔ, exact for a resonant drive without decay. At r = 4 the interaction is 4−6 ≈ 0.0002, far below Ω, so the pair is a product state and P(ab) is the product of the single-atom probabilities. Bitstrings list atom 1 on the left. The budget is the sum Σi εi|Δ| against the 2.27 of the AnalogDeviceWithDMM in canonical units; with one addressed atom it is ε|Δ|.
